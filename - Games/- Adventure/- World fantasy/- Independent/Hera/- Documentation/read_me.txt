GAME PLOT

The land of Hera was divided into three kingdoms. One day, the three kings decided to united, and created a unified council of ministers to rule all the land. The council was composed of ten wizards.

This reminds me of some real countries: the United Arab Emirates (where the seven monarchs are the president and the ministers), the United Kingdom of Great Britain (where the queen has less powers than the prime minister), Canada (the prime minister does everything, the queen does not even live in the country!), the United Crown of Denmark, etcetera...

Back to Hera, the prime wizard-minister, Zarebae, decided to overthrow the three kings and rule with the Council. The remaining nine ministers said "No!", and banned him. In exile, Zarebae organized an army of orcs and attacked the three united kingdoms.

One of the nine wizard-ministers, Rhin, manufactured an enchanted sword embedded with a magic crystal, and went to slay Zarebae. He failed, and the sword of Rhin was lost.

Later, a valiant noble horseman took up the quest. He failed: he left Zarebae's stronghold with the sword, but the evil wizard was alive, well, and in possession of the crystal.

It is now up to you (the player) to take up the quest: find the nobleman with the sword, and defeat Zarebae onca and for all.

Differences Between Apple II and DOS Versions

Although the Apple II and MS-DOS versions of Hera: The Sword of Rhin are largely the same game, there are a few gameplay differences noted by players:
 - Character Attribute Bonus: In the Apple II version, the third king you encounter will raise both of your attributes (Strength and Agility) when you earn enough experience, whereas in the DOS version this was changed so that the same king boosts only Strength. This means Apple II players can build up stats a bit more evenly at that point in the game.
 - Experience and Gold from Enemies: The DOS version tweaks the rewards from combat. Enemies in DOS give less experience and more gold compared to their Apple II counterparts. As a result, leveling up is significantly slower in DOS.
 - Final Dungeon Layout: The climactic area (Skull Mountain, Zarebae’s fortress) was made a bit simpler in the DOS port. On Apple II, Skull Mountain requires navigating two basement passages, one to retrieve the magic crystal and another to reach Zarebae, adding complexity to the final quest. In the DOS version, the dungeon design was streamlined so that there is essentially one continuous passage (after getting the crystal, the entrance corridor leads straight to Zarebae).
 
These differences, while not drastic, influence the gameplay pacing. Interestingly, veteran players have expressed a preference for the Apple II version due to its shorter grind and more intricate final dungeon. Other aspects – story, world content, puzzles, and controls – are virtually identical between the two platforms.
